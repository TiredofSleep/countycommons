# Grow-ops — low-energy growing units and their inputs

**Five growing units that feed into each other: greens, potatoes, chicken feed (fodder and insects), and on-site fertility. Each section gives measured figures and the source for each.**

## Energy

One estimate: growing indoors could take *"5, 10 or many more acres of solar panels to power each acre of vertical farming"* ([Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/food-and-farms/why-vertical-farming-just-doesnt-work)). The units below are passive or low-energy designs.

## Unit 1 — the greens unit

- **The Kratky method** is passive, non-circulating hydroponics invented at the University of Hawaiʻi (B.A. Kratky, CTAHR). A container of nutrient water and a net pot; the plant is watered by capillary action and an air gap feeds the roots — **no pump, no aerator, no electricity.** Leafy greens (lettuce, and spinach-type greens) reach harvest in **~6–7 weeks** ([Kratky / ISHS](https://www.ishs.org/ishs-article/843_6)).
- **A Deep Winter Greenhouse** is passive-solar: thermal mass stores the day's heat and only a small fan draws power. It grows greens through a cold winter ([UMN Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/growing-systems/deep-winter-greenhouses)).

## Unit 2 — potatoes

- **Towers.** Cultivariable, a grower-run reference site, concludes potato towers do not give better yields than growing under similar conditions without the extra hilling ([Cultivariable](https://www.cultivariable.com/potato-towers/)).
- **Grow bags.** We did not find a university source comparing grow-bag and in-ground yields.
- **Storage.** Cured potatoes (7–10 days), then held at **38–40°F and 85–90% humidity, keep up to 8 months** ([U Missouri MP562](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/mp562); [UAF Extension](https://www.uaf.edu/ces/publications/database/food/vegetable-storage.php)).

## Feeding the chickens

### Unit 3 — fodder / microgreens

Sprouting barley or wheat into a green mat takes ~6–8 days. Sprouting **loses 7–47% of the grain's dry matter** to respiration ([NDSU Extension](https://www.ndsu.edu/agriculture/extension/publications/feeding-value-sprouted-grains); [Feedipedia critical assessment](https://www.feedipedia.org/content/hydroponic-fodder-production-critical-assessment)), so the weight gain of fodder is water. NDSU: "there is a net decrease in available nutrients in sprouting the grain." Feedipedia estimates hydroponic fodder costs **3.4× more per kg of dry matter** than the grain. Alabama Extension advises feeding greens sparingly alongside a complete feed ([Alabama Extension](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/farming/backyard-small-poultry-flock-management-series-feeding-the-laying-hen/)).

### Unit 4 — insect protein

A PLOS ONE study ([Crickets Are Not a Free Lunch](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0118785)) found crickets on grain feed about 35% efficient, with **over 99% mortality on raw food scraps**. AAFCO has **no US poultry authorization for crickets**.

Black soldier fly larvae (BSFL):

- **~41.5% protein** (peer-reviewed average, [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9502457/)), raised on food scraps. A laying-hen trial fed **12 g live larvae/hen/day (~10% of intake) with no harm** and slightly better feed conversion ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7070753/)).
- **Legal status.** AAFCO's approved BSFL definition covers poultry **only when larvae are raised on certified feed-grade substrate**, not food scraps or manure ([AAFCO IDC minutes 2021](https://www.aafco.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Ingredient_Definitions_2021_Annual.pdf)).

## Fertility

- **Compost** is ~1-1-1 NPK ([UMass](https://www.umass.edu/agriculture-food-environment/vegetable/fact-sheets/compost-use-soil-fertility)); worm castings measure **1.94% N vs. 0.80%** for plain compost ([NMSU](https://pubs.nmsu.edu/_h/H164/)); black soldier fly **frass** measures **N≈3.2%, P≈1.2%, K≈2.9%** ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11050032/)). Composting cycles the nutrients already in the inputs; it does not create them.
- **Manure on food crops.** The USDA National Organic Program requires raw manure to be applied at least 90 days before harvest (120 days if the edible part touches the soil), or composted at 131–170°F (3 days aerated or 15 days windrow with 5 turns) ([7 CFR 205.203](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/section-205.203)).
- **Comfrey.** Comfrey leaf measured about 5.3% potassium in the Cornell/Unadilla trial ([USU Extension](https://extension.usu.edu/yardandgarden/research/comfrey-in-the-garden)). A Northeast SARE field trial found plant tissue tracks the soil it grows in, which does not support the claim that comfrey mines minerals from deep subsoil ([Northeast SARE / Cornell / Unadilla](https://projects.sare.org/project-reports/fne20-967/)).
- **Nitrogen.** Legume cover crops (clover, hairy vetch, field peas) fix **50–150 lb N/acre/year** from the atmosphere ([SARE](https://www.sare.org/publications/managing-cover-crops-profitably/legume-cover-crops/hairy-vetch/)).
- **Minerals.** Phosphorus, potassium, and calcium leave in exported food, and no atmospheric process replaces them ([Virginia Tech Extension](https://www.pubs.ext.vt.edu/CSES/CSES-98/CSES-98.html)).

## The grain question

Cereal grains are grass seeds: corn, wheat, barley, oats, sorghum, and millet are all in the grass family, Poaceae ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.95.5.2005)). Foraged pasture supplies only ~5–20% of a hen's diet; forage runs ~129–246 cal/lb against corn's ~1,632 ([NCAT/ATTRA](https://www.ncat.org/publication/pastured-poultry-nutrition-forages/)). A hen eats ~100–150 g/day at ~16–18% protein and 3–4% calcium ([Alabama Extension](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/farming/backyard-small-poultry-flock-management-series-feeding-the-laying-hen/)).

- **GM crop status.** In the US, commercial GM varieties exist for corn, soybeans, cotton, canola, alfalfa, and sugar beet (plus a few horticultural crops); sorghum, wheat, barley, oats, rye, millet, and rice have none ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/agricultural-biotechnology/gmo-crops-animal-food-and-beyond); [USDA ERS](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/charts-of-note/108187)).
- **Open-pollinated corn.** Varieties such as Reid's Yellow Dent and Bloody Butcher can be seed-saved with ~500 ft isolation ([Southern Exposure](https://blog.southernexposure.com/2022/04/whats-in-a-seed-open-pollinated-vs-hybrid-vs-gm/)).
- **Grain sorghum (milo).** Poultry Extension sources list it at 95–99% of corn's energy (~3,310 kcal/kg) ([poultry.extension.org](https://poultry.extension.org/articles/feeds-and-feeding-of-poultry/feed-ingredients-for-poultry/cereals-in-poultry-diets/feeding-sorghum-to-poultry/); [UA Extension](https://uaex.uada.edu/farm-ranch/crops-commercial-horticulture/grain-sorghum/)).
- **Pearl millet.** About 3,240 kcal/kg, and higher in methionine and lysine than corn ([poultry.extension.org](https://poultry.extension.org/articles/feeds-and-feeding-of-poultry/feed-ingredients-for-poultry/cereals-in-poultry-diets/pearl-millet-in-poultry-diets/)).
- **Methionine.** Grain-and-legume diets are short of the amino acid methionine. The USDA organic program permanently kept synthetic methionine in 2012 because natural sources need impractical volumes (1 lb synthetic ≈ 50 lb fishmeal; [NCAT/ATTRA](https://www.ncat.org/publication/organic-poultry-production-providing-adequate-methionine/)).
- **Calcium.** Recycled, coarsely crushed eggshell as a calcium source is supported in peer-reviewed work ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8225885/)).

## Seed types

Maize was reworked from teosinte over ~9,000 years ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1820997116)), so every crop here is human-shaped. Seed types differ in whether they can be saved and replanted:

| Seed that cannot be saved and replanted true | Seed that can |
|---|---|
| **Patented GE seed** — can't legally be saved (utility patents) | **Open-pollinated / heirloom** — breeds true from saved seed |
| **F1 hybrid** — a one-generation cross; offspring don't come true | **Landrace** — a diverse local population that adapts to local conditions over the years |

([UA Extension](https://www.uaex.uada.edu/counties/white/news/horticulture/F1-hybrid-seeds.aspx); [Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/blogs/good-growing/2025-10-10-open-pollinated-vs-hybrid-important-distinction-when-saving-seeds); [FAO](https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1251445/))

- **Saving seed.** Open-pollinated, heirloom, and landrace seed regrows from saved seed; hybrids and patented lines can't, biologically or legally ([UMD Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/legal-liability-saving-seeds-era-expiring-patents-fs-1000)).
- **Local adaptation.** Landraces carry more genetic diversity and are more stress- and drought-stable in low-input conditions, and yield less under ideal, high-input conditions ([Frontiers, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2025.1504922/full); [FAO](https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1251445/)).
- **Nutrients.** Breeding for yield can dilute mineral and protein concentration in some crops ([Davis 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15637215/)). Marles (2017) found the claim that food lost 40% of its nutrition since 1950 rests on confounded cross-decade comparisons ([Marles 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0889157516302113)).
- **Flavor.** Modern tomatoes lost sugars and aroma compounds to shipping-and-shelf-life breeding ([Molecular Plant](https://www.cell.com/molecular-plant/fulltext/S1674-2052(17)30131-4)).
- **Seed libraries.** The [Seed Savers Exchange](https://seedsavers.org/preservation/the-collection/) lends seed at planting and takes back a share at harvest; its collection holds 25,000+ varieties.
