# Food as a utility — growing it together

**A summary of how some governments support community food-growing, with the numbers and sources for each.**

Most food help is emergency help: a pantry box when a household has already run short. Some governments also support helping people *grow and share food together*, on public land and with Extension know-how. This page lists where that happens, with the numbers.

Feeding America's *Map the Meal Gap* publishes a food-insecurity rate for every US county ([Feeding America](https://map.feedingamerica.org/)).

## It already runs somewhere — with numbers

| The move | Where it already runs | The number |
|---|---|---|
| A **city** running community gardens as a public service | **Seattle P-Patch**, under the Dept. of Neighborhoods since 1973 | **91 gardens**; gardeners donated **40,000 lb** of produce to meal programs in 2023 ([seattle.gov](https://www.seattle.gov/neighborhoods/p-patch-gardening)) |
| A **county** running gardens through its Extension office | **Brown County, Wisconsin**, since 1996 | **13 gardens, 386 plots, 250+ gardeners**, funded jointly by the county, Extension, and the city ([UW–Madison Extension](https://brown.extension.wisc.edu/community-gardens/)) |
| Free land access without selling the land | **Newark's Adopt-a-Lot** ($1/year lease) and **Baltimore's** (free, revocable license) | A simple revocable license, no title transfer ([Newark](https://www.newarknj.gov/194/Adopt-a-Lot); [Baltimore](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/dhcd/developer-resident-resources/adopt-a-lot-program)) |
| A **statewide gleaning network** in every county | **Arkansas Gleaning Project** (AR Hunger Relief Alliance + Society of St. Andrew) | **12M+ lb** gleaned, **800+ orgs**, **all 75 counties** ([arhungeralliance.org](https://arhungeralliance.org/programs/ar-gleaning-project/)) |
| Free horticulture know-how in every AR county | **Arkansas Master Gardener Program** (UA System Extension) | **93,175 education + 184,443 service hours** logged statewide in a year ([uaex.uada.edu](https://www.uaex.uada.edu/yard-garden/master-gardeners/default.aspx)) |
| School gardens feeding cafeterias | **Denver "Garden to Cafeteria"** | **5,000+ lb** into salad bars at **~250 schools** ([Slow Food USA](https://slowfoodusa.org/school-gardens/garden-cafeteria/)) |
| A coordinating body housed in local government | **Food policy councils** (many set up by resolution) | **283 active in the US as of 2020** ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/issues/2025/24_0335.htm); [Johns Hopkins CLF](https://clf.jhsph.edu/stories/structuring-your-food-policy-council)) |

USDA funded this channel in Arkansas once: the 2011 **People's Garden School Pilot** partnered with the **University of Arkansas** to build school gardens through Cooperative Extension ([peoplesgarden.wsu.edu](https://peoplesgarden.wsu.edu/home/project-background/)).

USDA's Community Food Projects grant funds community food projects: up to $400,000, with a 1:1 match, applied for by a nonprofit ([USDA NIFA](https://www.nifa.usda.gov/grants/programs/hunger-food-security-programs/community-food-projects-competitive-grant-program-cfpcgp)).

## Larger programs elsewhere

**Belo Horizonte, Brazil** runs a "food as a right" system reported at roughly **2% of the city budget**, serving about **10,600 subsidized meals a day** ([NYC Food Policy Center, Hunter College](https://www.nycfoodpolicy.org/popular-restaurants-belo-horizonte-urban-food-policy-snapshot/)). **Rosario, Argentina** turned vacant land into **75 hectares** of gardens plus **800 hectares** preserved, **2,400+ families**, and **2,500 tons/year** — winning WRI's $250,000 Prize for Cities in 2021 ([WRI](https://www.wri.org/news/release-prize-cities-awarded-rosario-argentina-improving-resilience-and-equity-through-urban)). **Incredible Edible** started in one English town in 2008 planting free-to-pick food in public beds and now reports **120+ UK groups and 700+ worldwide** ([incredibleedible.org.uk](https://www.incredibleedible.org.uk/our-story/)).

## What the evidence says about limits

1. **Garden yields.** Algert et al. (2014, *J. Acad. Nutr. Diet.*) estimated a California plot yields about **$435 of produce a season** (widely cited; primary abstract paywalled).
2. **Health evidence.** The best systematic review (Hume et al. 2022, *BMC Public Health*) finds community gardening associated with higher fruit-and-vegetable intake, and states that "evidence quality overall was low" ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9229094/)).
3. **Community fridges.** The Freedge network (150+ US fridges as of 2021, [Shelterforce](https://shelterforce.org/2021/02/24/community-fridges-provide-vital-and-visible-relief-in-the-war-on-hunger/)) is run by neighbors, not by a government program.
