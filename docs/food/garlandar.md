# Food as a utility — growing it together

**A summary of how some governments support community food-growing, with the numbers and sources for each.**

Most food help is emergency help: a pantry box when a household has already run short. Some governments also support helping people *grow and share food together*. This page lists where that happens, with the numbers, and what already exists in Garland County.

Feeding America's 2023 estimate puts Garland County food insecurity at about **20%** ([Aspire Arkansas](https://www.aspirearkansas.org/families/food-insecurity)); the CHI St. Vincent Hot Springs 2025 Community Health Needs Assessment cites the 2022 figure at **19.6%**, and the **child** rate at **26.2%** ([CHNA](https://www.commonspirit.org/content/dam/commonspiritorg/en/chisv/soar/marketing/docs/pdfs/community/2025-hot-springs-chna-report.pdf)). (The two county rates differ only by data year — 2022 vs. 2023 — of the same Feeding America dataset.)

## What Garland County already has

| The piece | What's already here | Source |

## It already runs as a public service elsewhere — with numbers

- **Seattle P-Patch** — 91 community gardens run by a city department since 1973; gardeners donated 40,000 lb of produce to meal programs in 2023 ([seattle.gov](https://www.seattle.gov/neighborhoods/p-patch-gardening)).
- **Brown County, Wisconsin** — 13 gardens, 386 plots run through its Extension office, the county, and the city since 1996 ([UW Extension](https://brown.extension.wisc.edu/community-gardens/)).
- **Newark's Adopt-a-Lot** ($1/year) and **Baltimore's** (free) — a simple revocable license lets a government open land for gardens without selling it ([Newark](https://www.newarknj.gov/194/Adopt-a-Lot)).
- **Arkansas Gleaning Project** — 12M+ lb gleaned, in all 75 counties ([arhungeralliance.org](https://arhungeralliance.org/programs/ar-gleaning-project/)).
- USDA's Community Food Projects grant funds community food projects: up to $400,000, with a 1:1 match, applied for by a nonprofit ([USDA NIFA](https://www.nifa.usda.gov/grants/programs/hunger-food-security-programs/community-food-projects-competitive-grant-program-cfpcgp)).

## Larger programs elsewhere

**Belo Horizonte, Brazil** runs a "food as a right" system reported at ~2% of its city budget, serving ~10,600 subsidized meals a day ([NYC Food Policy Center](https://www.nycfoodpolicy.org/popular-restaurants-belo-horizonte-urban-food-policy-snapshot/)). **Rosario, Argentina** turned vacant land into 75 hectares of gardens, 2,400+ families, and 2,500 tons/year — winning WRI's Prize for Cities in 2021 ([WRI](https://www.wri.org/news/release-prize-cities-awarded-rosario-argentina-improving-resilience-and-equity-through-urban)).

## What the evidence says about limits

1. **Garden yields.** Algert et al. (2014) estimated a California plot yields about $435 of produce a season (widely cited; primary abstract paywalled).
2. **Health evidence.** The best systematic review (Hume et al. 2022, *BMC Public Health*) links community gardening to higher fruit-and-vegetable intake and states that "evidence quality overall was low" ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9229094/)).
3. **Community fridges.** The Freedge network (150+ US fridges as of 2021, [Shelterforce](https://shelterforce.org/2021/02/24/community-fridges-provide-vital-and-visible-relief-in-the-war-on-hunger/)) is run by neighbors, not by a government program.
