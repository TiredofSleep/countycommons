# The grower's almanac — year-round food in Arkansas

**How to make food come out of the ground twelve months a year in central Arkansas (roughly USDA zone 8a), so a community fridge, hub, and cellar never go empty.** Built on University of Arkansas Cooperative Extension planting data. Every date is a regional average — a guide, not a guarantee; always check the current forecast.

**Climate baseline (Garland County / Hot Springs):** USDA hardiness **zone 8a**; average last spring frost **~April 3**, first fall frost **~October 31** (~211 frost-free days, NWS Little Rock, 1991–2020). Clark County (Arkadelphia) is effectively the same.

The point isn't a summer garden — it's a supply that never stops. That takes three habits most gardens skip: **plant a fall garden in the heat of August, grow crops bred to store, and keep the cold-hardy greens going through winter under cover.**

## The year, month by month

| Month | Plant & start | Harvest | Store & do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | Garlic (last call, by mid-Jan); start onion seed | Kale, collards, spinach, carrots — under cover | Soil-test; order seed; build beds with compost |
| Feb | Irish potatoes (mid-Feb); onion sets; peas, spinach, lettuce, radish, carrots, beets, kale; broccoli/cabbage starts | Overwintered greens | Row cover for early sowings |
| Mar | More cool-season; start tomatoes & peppers indoors | Early greens, radish, spinach | Harden off transplants |
| Apr | *After ~Apr 3 frost:* beans, sweet corn, tomatoes; sweet-potato slips (Apr 20+) | Spring greens, lettuce, radish, green onions | Mulch beds; trellises |
| May | Okra, peppers, eggplant, cucumbers, squash, melons, lima beans | Peas, lettuce, strawberries, spring onions | Succession-sow beans & corn |
| Jun | Southern peas; successions of beans, squash, corn | Squash, cucumbers, dry onions, early tomatoes, new potatoes | Keep water steady |
| Jul | Fall starts: tomatoes (Jul 1–15), corn, southern peas, summer squash; fall potatoes (Jul 15+) | Garlic, tomatoes, peppers, squash, beans, melons | Cure garlic |
| Aug | **The fall garden** (Aug 1–Sep 15): carrots, collards, broccoli/cabbage/cauliflower, beets, chard, turnips, mustard; kale (20th+), spinach (25th+), lettuce | Summer peak | Start fall transplants |
| Sep | Finish fall greens & roots (through 15th); last spinach, lettuce, radish | Summer tail + first fall greens | Set low tunnels / row cover |
| Oct | Garlic (start); overwintering onions | *Before frost:* winter squash, sweet potatoes; fall greens ramping | Cure sweet potatoes & squash |
| Nov | Garlic (still fine); overwinter onions | Fall peak: kale, collards, spinach, carrots, beets, broccoli, cabbage | Onions & potatoes into the cellar; cover winter beds |
| Dec | Garlic (still fine) | Cold-hardy greens & roots under cover — sweeter after frost | Plan next year; turn compost |

## Winter is the whole game

Most gardens quit in October; the ones that feed a community don't. In zone 8a, **kale, collards, spinach, mustard, and turnip greens** keep producing through winter, **garlic and overwintered onions** hold in the ground until summer, and **carrots and beets** keep under mulch. A **floating row cover** buys about 2°F of protection (heavier fabric 6°F+); a **low tunnel or cold frame** buys more.

**The season-extension move that's capital, not labor — a hoop house.** A high tunnel turns three seasons into four, and the **USDA NRCS Seasonal High Tunnel cost-share** helps pay for the structure (a grant that buys ten years of growing — see [the engine](/engine)). Rules: pre-manufactured kit, ≤30 ft wide, ≥6 ft peak, ≤2,160 sq ft, grown in native soil; you front the cost and get reimbursed. Get the current Arkansas rate from your local NRCS office.

## The cellar crops — grown to store

| Crop | Cure | Store at | Keeps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sweet potatoes | 85°F, 90% humidity, 7–10 days | 55–60°F, 85–90% humidity | 6–8 months |
| Irish potatoes | 60°F, a few days, dark | 38–42°F, ~90% humidity | 6–8 months |
| Winter squash & pumpkins | harvest before hard frost | 55–60°F, dry | 2–6 months |
| Garlic | dry down, airflow | 72°F+, dry (not cold) | ~8 months |
| Onions | dry down | 32°F, 65–70% humidity | 6–7 months |
| Dry beans | dry fully; freeze 48h to kill weevils | cool, dry, in jars | 1 year+ |

Two habits worth the ink: **don't store onions and potatoes together** (each speeds the other's spoiling), and onions sprout in a fridge — keep them cold-cold or cool-dry, not in between.

## Free dirt, free help

- **Free compost:** the City of Hot Springs Compost Facility (318 Davidson Dr) offers free self-loading compost/mulch to city residents and utility customers.
- **Free know-how:** your county Cooperative Extension office offers free soil testing, plant/pest ID, and the Master Gardener program, whose volunteers staff county-sanctioned community and school gardens.
