# SCOPE.md — Narrow trunk, wide grafts, in trust order

Decided 2026-08-08 after researching both models. The question: stay narrow
(government money + a place to talk about it) or go wide (recycling drives,
community project funding, awareness, the whole commons)?

## The evidence

- **Front Porch Forum (Vermont)** proves the wide community platform works at
  town scale: 200 local forums, 81% of members feel more informed there vs.
  26% on Facebook (New_ Public study, 13k+ members). Its engine is "move
  slowly and moderate heavily" — a paid moderation staff. That collides with
  this platform's under-3-hours/week constraint. Its design lessons (no
  algorithm, no likes, no reply dogpiles, strictly local) are already ours.
- **Civic crowdfunding (ioby, Spacehive)** funds parks, gardens, cleanups —
  and governments join in with match funding, building trust rather than
  spending it. Community project money with receipts is this platform's
  native language.
- **The civic-tech graveyard** kills platforms two ways: scope before
  adoption, and moderation load. The charter's bright lines and the
  maintenance ceiling are the survival constraints.

## The decision

1. **The trunk is narrow and first: money + voice + help.** Budget trail,
   open questions, help finder — through the first closed loop. This is the
   thing no one else does, and it is what officials can safely cite.
2. **Cheap neutral grafts, soon:** a civic calendar/bulletin — every public
   body's meetings plus community listings (recycling drives, cleanups,
   blood drives) as factual listings with no discussion threads. Front Porch
   Forum's informational value at zero moderation cost. Recycling as a
   money-trail page ("where does the sanitation fund's recycling actually
   go") belongs to the trunk itself.
3. **The big graft, after the first closed loop: Commons Projects** —
   ioby-style community funding of local projects with open books per
   project, every dollar to the receipt, officials invited to match.
   Rally energy that builds trust instead of spending it. This is the
   feature that earns the name County Commons.
4. **The never (charter-level): the platform does not host protest
   organizing.** Awareness lives here as checkable facts, open questions,
   and the traction rule. Organizing migrates to tools built for it. The
   Trust Ladder — officials citing, then contributing — cannot coexist with
   being the place marches are organized; one protest hosted here prices
   every packet after it at zero.

One sentence: **narrow in what it opposes (nothing), wide in what it enables
(everything a county does together), sequenced by trust.**
