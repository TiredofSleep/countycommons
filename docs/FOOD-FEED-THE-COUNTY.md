# What it would take to feed a county

**A model of the calories, land and labor needed to feed a given number of people, using USDA and Extension figures. Figures are shown per 1,000 people so they can be scaled to any county.**

Every building-block figure is cited below. The totals are simple arithmetic on those figures, and the assumptions are stated in the open. The ranges are wide on purpose.

## What one person needs a year

- **Calories:** ~2,000 kcal/day, or **~730,000 kcal/year** ([USDA Dietary Guidelines](https://www.dietaryguidelines.gov/) reference; real needs run 1,600–3,200/day by age and activity).
- **Vegetables:** the US eats **~414 lbs of vegetables and pulses per person a year** ([USDA ERS food availability](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-availability-per-capita-data-system/)).
- **Protein:** 46 g/day (women) to 56 g/day (men) ([National Academies Dietary Reference Intakes](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/10490/dietary-reference-intakes-for-energy-carbohydrate-fiber-fat-fatty-acids-cholesterol-protein-and-amino-acids)).

For **1,000 people**, the vegetables alone come to **~414,000 pounds a year**.

## Land and labor, by scope

### Vegetables and fruit

- **Labor.** The SARE market-garden study of 19 growers found average labor of just under 2,000 hours per acre on farms under 3 acres and just under 850 hours per acre on 3–12 acre farms ([SARE LNC01-194](https://projects.sare.org/sare_project/lnc01-194/)). Divided into a 2,000-hour work year, that is roughly 0.4 to 1 full-time worker per acre.

### Calories from staples

- **Land.** Potatoes yield about 15.3 million calories per acre (see yield data in [USDA NASS Quick Stats](https://quickstats.nass.usda.gov/)). At 730,000 kcal per person a year, that is about 0.05 acre per person, or about **48 acres per 1,000 people**, if the diet were all potatoes. A staple mix (potatoes, sweet potatoes, beans, winter squash, grain) would need more; this model puts it at roughly 100–190 acres per 1,000 people.

### The whole current diet

- **Land.** Peters et al. (2016) report about **1.08 hectares (2.7 acres) per person** for the baseline American diet and about **0.13 hectares (0.3 acre) per person** for the most land-efficient diet they modeled. That is roughly **2,700 acres per 1,000 people** versus **320 acres per 1,000 people** ([Peters et al. 2016](https://elementascience.org/articles/116)).
- To compare with a county, multiply by its population and compare with its acres in farms in the [USDA Census of Agriculture county profile](https://www.nass.usda.gov/). The [local food production](/sovereignty) page gives the same diet figures.

## The caveats

- **Ranges are wide** because intensive and conventional production differ by 5–10×; the labor figures come from a real but non-random 19-farm study (SARE) and are directional, not a national average.
- **Diet drives everything.** The land numbers swing enormously with how much meat is in the diet.
- **Staple yields** use USDA national or regional figures (the sweet-potato yield is a Mississippi proxy) from [USDA NASS Quick Stats](https://quickstats.nass.usda.gov/); Arkansas-specific yields would refine the staples estimate.
