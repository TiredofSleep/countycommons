# The frontier — new-age growing methods and what they use

**Measured inputs (energy, labor, yield) for newer growing methods, with sources.**

## Container farms

A shipping-container farm (Freight Farms and similar) grows leafy greens year-round with little labor. Freight Farms' published specs list **151–350 kWh a day** per unit, and a few tons of greens a year ([Freight Farms specs](https://www.freightfarms.com/greenery-s)). That is about 55,000–128,000 kWh a year; the average US residential customer bought 10,791 kWh in 2022 ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=97&t=3)), so one unit uses about 5–12 households' worth of electricity.

Industry-wide, vertical farms use **~38.8 kWh per kg** of food vs. ~5.4 for an unlit greenhouse ([Agritecture / 2021 CEA Census](https://www.agritecture.com/blog/2022/6/10/vertical-farms-have-the-vision-but-do-they-have-the-energy)). A peer-reviewed 2025 analysis found indoor farming converts electricity to food at about **1% efficiency** and is **10–100× too expensive for staple calories**, and that it only pencils for high-value leafy greens ([*Plant Physiology* 2025](https://academic.oup.com/plphys/article/198/3/kiaf056/8104144)).

## Potato towers

Cultivariable, a grower-run reference site, reviewed potato towers in detail and concludes they do not give better yields than growing under similar conditions without the extra hilling, because potatoes set tubers near the seed piece and the lowest part of the stem ([Cultivariable](https://www.cultivariable.com/potato-towers/)).

## Other methods and their measured effects

| Strategy | Labor effect | Energy and inputs |
|---|---|---|
| **Container / vertical farm** | Little labor | 151–350 kWh/day per unit (Freight Farms); ~38.8 kWh/kg (CEA Census) |
| **Potato towers** | Extra work to build and hill | No yield gain reported (Cultivariable) |
| **No-till** | **30–50% less labor** ([USDA ARS](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/20902500/DavidHuggins/NoTill.pdf)) | Less diesel and equipment use |
| **Passive-solar season extension** (deep-winter greenhouse) | Greens through cold months | Passive solar heat and thermal mass, with a small fan ([UMN Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/growing-systems/deep-winter-greenhouses)) |
