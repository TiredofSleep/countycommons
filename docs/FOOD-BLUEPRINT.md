# The $400K blueprint — spend a big grant to last

**What one USDA-scale grant can build for a county food system, if every dollar is spent to last.** $400,000 is the real ceiling of a USDA Community Food Projects grant — the plausible big check. Spent as payroll it buys about three years and then dies. Spent as **capital** — assets that keep producing — plus a single, *shrinking* operator bridge that hands off to earned revenue, it buys the middle of a food economy that outlives the grant.

> **Grants buy capital, once. Sales buy operations, forever.**

Every line below is a durable asset, a leverage play, or a time-boxed bridge — nothing recurring the grant can't renew. **The dollar figures are planning estimates; get real quotes before committing.** The lines marked (*) especially need live quotes.

## Make $400K spend like $600K

Every dollar should drag in a dollar of leverage: **NRCS EQIP** cost-share pays roughly $5.90–$12.21/sq ft toward each hoop house (often $8,000–$10,000, enough to cover most of a structure), so the county fund pays mainly for site prep. An **existing certified kitchen** beats a six-figure build. A **used** reefer and **CoolBot** cold rooms (~$3k vs. $6–10k for a walk-in) stretch the cold-chain dollars. **Workforce funds** pay the apprentices, not this grant.

## The 3-year handoff is the whole trick

- **Year 1** — build the middle and land the first anchor buyer. Grant-heavy (~90%).
- **Year 2** — sales cover about half; the operator's bridge steps down.
- **Year 3** — anchor contracts + produce prescriptions + value-added margin carry operations (~85% earned). The grant is gone and the cooler, tunnels, van, and kitchen remain.

## Buy the bottleneck, not the postcard

The constraint on a local food economy is not growing — people can grow. The choke point is the **middle**: aggregation, cold storage, processing, and *delivery* — the boring infrastructure that lets a small grower reach a hospital kitchen that pays. Almost the whole budget buys that middle, because that is what unlocks the anchor demand that funds everything after.

## And the version we'll probably actually start with

The same system at ~$0 is a stall at the year-round farmers market, gleaning and a donated fridge into the pantry, and a church-lot garden fed by free compost — selling what it can, giving what it can't, earning its way toward the assets. **The $400K doesn't invent the system; it fast-forwards it a decade** — straight to the cold chain and the middle instead of the years it would take to earn them.

## Read before you believe the number

- **The operator bridge (~34%) is the risk** — the one line that isn't a hard asset. Deliberately front-loaded and shrinking, but land an anchor buyer *before* year 3, not after.
- **Several numbers need a real quote, not a web search** — the used reefer van (least-confirmed), the kitchen fit-out (site-specific), the current Arkansas NRCS rate, the food-hub startup budget.
- **It needs a business operator, and anchor contracts reward reliability** — a 3–5 year build, not a season.
