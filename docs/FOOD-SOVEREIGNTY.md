# The path to food sovereignty

**Research on local food production at the scale of a county.**

Food sovereignty is *"the right of peoples to healthy and culturally appropriate food produced through ecologically sound and sustainable methods, and their right to define their own food and agriculture systems"* — the food system serving the people who grow and eat, [La Via Campesina](https://viacampesina.org/en/what-is-food-sovereignty/) put it, "rather than the demands of markets and corporations."

## How much land different diets need (Peters et al., 2016)

- In the diet closest to how America eats now, **only ~20% of cropland grows food for people — about 80% grows feed and forage for animals** (Peters et al., *Elementa* 2016, a peer-reviewed carrying-capacity study).
- The same study found the baseline diet needs an **eight-fold** greater land footprint than the most land-efficient (plant-forward) diet — roughly **1.08 vs. 0.13 hectares per person** (about 2.7 vs. 0.3 acres) ([Peters et al. 2016](https://elementascience.org/articles/116); [Tufts summary](https://now.tufts.edu/2016/07/22/us-land-capacity-feeding-people-could-expand-dietary-changes)).

For a county's own numbers, multiply these per-person figures by the county's population and compare with its acres in farms in the [USDA Census of Agriculture county profile](https://www.nass.usda.gov/). The [feeding the county](/feed) page shows that arithmetic side by side.

## Feed and egg figures

- Beef takes about **6 lb of grain per pound of beef** (or 7–25 lb of forage), versus **2.5 lb of feed per pound of chicken** ([Noble Research Institute](https://www.noble.org/regenerative-agriculture/livestock/the-efficiency-of-beef-production/)).
- A laying hen lays **250–300 eggs a year** ([UNH Extension](https://extension.unh.edu/resource/producing-your-own-eggs)).
- Arkansas hunters take about **200,000 deer a year** ([AGFC](https://www.agfc.com/news/arkansas-deer-harvest-increases-just-short-of-200000/)).
- Beans and pulses have about **1/13th the carbon per gram of protein of beef** ([Drewnowski & Conrad 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11377338/)).

## Precedents

- **In 1943, victory gardens grew 42% of all the produce in the United States** — from more than **20 million** household and community gardens, about 10 billion pounds of food ([National WWII Museum, citing USDA](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/victory-gardens-world-war-ii)).
- After the Soviet trade collapse cut Cuba off from food imports in the 1990s, its cities grew food on neighborhood plots and organopónicos. Havana's urban-farming workforce grew from 9,000 to over 44,000, and its urban farms are reported (FAO and Cuban data, via economist Sinan Koont, *Sustainable Urban Agriculture in Cuba*, University Press of Florida, 2011) to have supplied **somewhere between half and the large majority of Havana's fresh vegetables**. These figures are secondary-sourced and given as a range.

## Community land trusts

A community land trust is a nonprofit that holds land and leases it long-term to farmers (the Agrarian Trust model). The first US example, New Communities Inc., lost its land in a 1985 foreclosure ([CLT case study](https://www.cltweb.org/resources/clt-case-studies/new-communities/)).

Residents have started petitions on ideas like this — see [petitions](/petitions).
