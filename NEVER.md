# NEVER.md — What This Project Will Never Do

*CustomPOS and its family (Open Books, training mode, and the sponsored civic work). Written July 2026, deliberately before anyone was asking — because a list like this is easy to write when nothing is at stake and politically impossible to write after success arrives. If you are reading this because the project got big: this document predates the temptation, and that is the point. Hold us to it.*

## Money

**Free stays free.** The software, every vertical template, the training system, and the operations knowledge that ships with it. No paid tiers, no premium features, no "community edition" bait with an enterprise upsell behind it. The project's costs are covered by its sponsor and by an optional, disclosed payment-processing referral — and that referral's economics are published, not buried in a margin.

**No promises with clocks in them.** No SLAs, no uptime guarantees, no support contracts, no enterprise deals, no commitments that make anyone's emergency into our emergency. This software is built to degrade softly and be maintained lightly — by design, so that it can stay free and its builder can stay sane. If your business requires guaranteed response times, this is honestly the wrong tool, and we'd rather say so here than fail you later.

**No ads, no selling data, no monetizing attention.** The register is not a surveillance device and its users are not inventory.

## Data and persons

**Ledgers get lit; persons stay dark.** Institutional and business transparency is the mission; individual human beings keep their privacy. No feature will ever publish an identifiable person's wages, purchases, debts, votes, or finances. Aggregation floors are code, not settings.

**Transparency is voluntary for humans, structural for institutions.** Open Books will never default to public, never be a condition of using the software, and never become a tool for anyone to demand another person's disclosure — an employer of a worker, a landlord of a tenant. The direction of the light matters: it points at ledgers and institutions, never down at people.

**No competitor data circles.** Shops that publish do so one-way, to the general public. The platform will never build private benchmarking or data-sharing between identifiable competitors — public disclosure is pro-competitive; private exchange between rivals is where collusion lives, and we will not host it.

## Integrity of the numbers

**Provenance over theater.** Published figures render from the operating system that produced them — numbers that fall out of the till, not out of marketing. The project will never add features that let a shop curate a prettier public picture than its own register knows: no manual overrides of published totals, no deleting history. Corrections and true-ups are displayed with their deltas, never overwritten.

## The civic work

**No candidates, no ballot measures, no named individuals — ever.** The sponsored civic platform computes and cites; it never advocates, never endorses, never displays official endorsements, and never becomes opposition infrastructure. These lines tighten, not loosen, in election seasons.

## The people

**The builder is not the product.** If attention arrives, the artifacts speak and the pitch gets declined — the same doctrine applied to county judges and to journalists alike: deliver results, ask for nothing, stay at the counter most mornings. No growth obligation, no roadmap owed to anyone, no version of success that requires the founder to become necessary rather than less necessary.

**Success is capability, then release.** The project is finished when it is capable of its target, not when it has captured a market. If a better steward — a community, a co-op, even a government — someday absorbs this work and makes it standard, that is the win condition, not a loss.
